Abandoned but Not Forgotten

Every mission leaves something behind. Every object has a story.

Across the Moon and Mars, NASA missions have left behind spacecraft, scientific instruments and exploration equipment. Though their missions have ended, their stories and contributions to science remain.

About the Project

A short guide to what this website is and why these objects matter.

What the challenge is about

"Abandoned but Not Forgotten" is a NASA Space Apps challenge about telling the stories of NASA equipment, devices and spacecraft left behind on the Moon or Mars.

Why equipment stays behind

Each object was left for a reason connected to its mission. Eagle's descent stage stayed on the Moon when the astronauts left in the ascent stage, and the Apollo 15 rover was parked when the astronauts lifted off. Surveyor 3, Viking 1 and Opportunity stopped communicating with Earth and remain where they worked.

Why they are interesting

Each object marks an important moment: the first crewed landing on the Moon, the first vehicle driven on another world, the first successful U.S. landing on Mars, a lander visited by astronauts more than two years after it landed, and a rover that worked for almost 15 years.

How this project helps

Every object has its own four-part story, photographs with credits, a timeline and links to NASA sources. A short quiz lets you check what you learned.

Why This Matters

Even when a mission ends, the knowledge gained from it remains valuable.

Their records, photographs and discoveries remain part of the story of space exploration. Telling their stories helps make sure they are not forgotten.
